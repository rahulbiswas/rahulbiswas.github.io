Marrakesh Deep Dive: The Final 3 Riads

A close look at the three finalists for Thu June 17 – Sun June 20, 2027 (3 nights, 2 adults + kids 13 & 6): rooms, photos, recent reviews, exact locations, and how arrival and departure will work on your first trip to Morocco. All three are well under the $2,700 budget.

Arrive: BA 2812 lands RAK 6:15 PM Thu Leave: ONCF train to Fès 8:35 AM Sun Budget: $2,700 First-timer logistics ↓

Bottom line

  • For a first visit with kids, Dar Num ($2,135) is the strongest pick. It has the most space, the easiest car access, very effective AC, and the best record with families, including first-timers. Before booking, confirm that Najib can meet you around 8 PM and help you leave at 7:30 AM Sunday, since both are outside staff hours.
  • Boutique Riad Medina ($1,160) is the proven value: 268 reviews and a smooth concierge arrival, for $975 less than Dar Num. The catch is that the third bedroom is on a hot rooftop with no railings.
  • Riad Kaolane ($1,270) is the most central and gives everyone a room, with guaranteed check-in until 10 PM. It's also the newest, with only 11 reviews and the longest walk from the car.

Where it is

Riad Laarous neighborhood in the north Medina, a block from Ben Youssef Madrasa (Airbnb shows only an approximate pin). The host says Jemaa el-Fna is about a 10-minute walk. Guests describe the lanes as quiet, with a petit-taxi rank and an ATM nearby. Nearby: Le Jardin Secret, Dar El Bacha (Bacha Coffee), Maison de la Photographie, and Restaurant Limoni around the corner.

Getting from the car to the door

The riad's driver (€25) drops you at the entrance to the street, where Chaouki meets you and loads your bags on a cart. The host sends a Google Maps pin and the concierge's phone number, so your driver can call him directly. Reviewers consistently call check-in smooth, including one guest whose arrival was delayed by lost luggage.

Who sleeps where

Two bedrooms are on the main floors and the third is on the roof terrace, next to the outdoor shower. A natural split: you two and Ellie on the lower floors, Rohan in the rooftop room. One family's 11-year-old happily slept up there alone.

What guests love

  • The rooftop bedroom can be hot at night in summer. A July guest brought portable fans

First Time in Morocco: How the Three Days Will Actually Go

Thu Jun 17 · 6:15 PM
Land at Marrakesh Menara (RAK) on BA 2812. Evening immigration lines can take 30–60+ minutes. After customs, use an ATM in the arrivals hall for dirhams. The riad's driver waits with a name sign. It's a 20–25 minute drive, then staff meet you at the drop-off point and walk you in. Expect to reach the riad around 8:00–8:30 PM. Pre-order dinner at the riad for that night; it's the easiest first evening, and guests at all three rave about the home cooking.
Fri Jun 18 & Sat Jun 19
Work around the heat. Head out by 8:30–9 AM for sights and souks, come back around 1 PM for lunch, the pool, and a rest while it's hottest, then go out again around 5:30 PM for Jemaa el-Fna at sunset and dinner. On Friday, some souk shops close around midday prayers. For Jardin Majorelle and the YSL Museum (a ~15-minute drive to Guéliz), book timed tickets online and go early.
Sun Jun 20 · 7:30 AM
Leave for the 8:35 AM ONCF train to Fès. Marrakesh station is in Guéliz, a 10–15 minute drive. Breakfast normally starts at 8:30 at all three riads, so ask for an early or packed breakfast and a car at 7:30. Staff will walk your bags out to it.

💵 Money

  • The currency is the dirham (MAD), roughly 10 to the dollar. You generally can't get it before you go, so use the airport ATM.
  • Nicer restaurants take cards. Taxis, tips, and souk shopping are cash, so keep small notes.
  • Typical tips: 10–20 MAD per bag for luggage carts; 50–100 MAD per day for riad staff, given at the end; 100–200 MAD for a half-day guide; about 10% at restaurants.

📱 Phones

  • Get an eSIM with data before you fly.
  • Riad staff and drivers coordinate on WhatsApp. Save the manager's number and the riad's map pin, and send both to your driver.

🚶 Getting around the Medina

  • Most lanes are too narrow for cars, but motorbikes zip through, so keep Ellie on the inside.
  • The lanes are a maze. Download an offline map and save the riad's pin.
  • Locals may offer directions ("that way is closed") and then ask for money. Say "La, shukran" (no, thank you) and keep walking.
  • A licensed guide on the first morning, booked through the riad, takes the stress out of the souks.
  • Petit taxis take at most 3 passengers. A family of 4 needs two, so have the riad book a car or van instead.

☀️ Heat & health

  • Mid-June highs are typically in the mid-30s °C (mid-90s °F), and heat waves can pass 40°C.
  • Drink bottled water only. Bring hats and sunscreen.
  • All three riads have AC in every bedroom.

🐍 Jemaa el-Fna with kids

  • It's best at sunset.
  • Snake charmers, monkey handlers, and henna artists expect payment for photos or if they approach you.
  • Skip "black henna" for the kids, since it can cause skin burns. Natural henna is brown or orange.
  • Keep phones and wallets in front pockets.

🕌 Customs, entry & calendar

  • Modest clothes (covered shoulders and knees) are respectful in the Medina.
  • Non-Muslims can't enter most mosques, so you'll see the Koutoubia from outside. The Ben Youssef Madrasa is open to visitors.
  • US passports need no visa for stays up to 90 days.
  • Your dates fall well clear of Ramadan (Feb–Mar 2027) and Eid al-Adha (mid-May 2027).
