1. What the Cotswolds Is & Why It Looks Like a Storybook

Honey-Gold Jurassic Stone

An 800-square-mile Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty 1.5 hours west of London. Everything sits on a vein of Jurassic oolitic limestone—every cottage, church, pub, and dry-stone wall is built from the same local stone that weathers to a warm honey-gold.

Frozen in Time by Wool

In the Middle Ages, sheep on these rolling hills ("wolds") produced Europe's finest wool, funding grand limestone manors and "wool churches." When the Industrial Revolution shifted north to coal country, the Cotswolds was bypassed—preserving its 16th- and 17th-century villages intact.

Highlights for the Four of You

  • Bourton-on-the-Water Model Village: A 1930s 1:9 scale replica of the village carved out of real Cotswold stone (with a miniature model village inside the model village).
  • Lower & Upper Slaughter: A quiet 1-mile footpath along the tiny River Eye with stone footbridges and zero tour buses.
  • Cotswold Wildlife Park: Walk at eye level with giraffes and past rhinos on the lawns of a Victorian manor near Burford.
